# Analytics methodology

## SUMMARY
## Analytics methodology: what the evidence supports

**Bottom line:** The engine is worth building, but the user's pitch overstates what calculation can do. The evidence supports five things that add real value: (1) an equal-weight consensus of stat-level projections, rescored to each league's exact scoring; (2) a probability-of-playing (P(play)) model built from injury designation and practice status; (3) league-aware replacement levels and marginal lineup value for trades and waivers; (4) usage and expected-points regression signals, such as expected TDs (xTD) for buy-low/sell-high; and (5) Vegas implied team totals as the main matchup and game-environment signal. Three things are mostly noise: WR-vs-CB shadow adjustments, man/zone splits, and individual "injury risk" scores. At most they belong in small, capped, clearly labeled experimental features.

### 1. Consensus
- **Projections beat rankings.** Fantasy Football Analytics (FFA) found averaged projections had R² ≈ 0.53 against actual results, versus 0.27 for crowd rankings.
- **Blending works, fancy weighting doesn't.** Blended projections beat every single source. A simple mean was about as good as accuracy-weighted means and slightly better than robust means. This matches the "forecast combination puzzle" (Smith & Wallis 2009): weights estimated from small samples rarely beat equal weights.
- **Method:** rescore each source's stat line to the league's Sleeper `scoring_settings`, then average with equal weights. Bonus scoring (e.g., 100-yard bonuses) is nonlinear, so its expected value has to come from the outcome distribution, not the mean.
- **Rankings:** use FantasyPros ECR (Expert Consensus Rankings, built from Rank Points; nflverse `load_ff_rankings()` exposes `ecr`, `sd`, `best`, `worst`). Convert ranks to points by mapping onto the same week's position projection curve, and use `sd` as a disagreement signal.
- **Missing sources:** treat a missing projection as possibly informative, since sources tend to omit players they think won't play.

### 2. Distributions and win probability
- Weekly scoring is right-skewed and has a point mass at zero (inactive or early exit).
- **Model:** P(play) × an outcome distribution whose spread depends on position and projected mean, fitted from projection errors.
- **Typical coefficient of variation (CV):** QB lowest (about 0.4); RB 0.5–0.6; WR 0.6–0.7; TE 0.7–0.8.
- **Correlations:** QB–WR1 about 0.46–0.55; QB vs. opposing defense/special teams strongly negative.

**How much does optimizing for win probability add?** My own normal-approximation calculation: in a typical 9-starter PPR matchup, the gap between the two team scores has an SD of about 32 points. Swapping a low-variance player for a high-variance one with the same mean changes win probability by only +0.2 to +0.7 percentage points (pp). The swap is only worth giving up 0.5 projected points when you are a 20+ point underdog. A 10,000-run Monte Carlo has about 0.5 pp standard error, which is as large as the effect itself.
- **So:** the MVP maximizes expected points, with P(play) built in. Win-probability optimization comes in v1, using common random numbers or an analytic approximation, mainly as a tiebreaker and a displayed number.

### 3. Usage and regression
- **Expected fantasy points (xFP):** ffverse `ffopportunity` is an xgboost model trained on 2006–2020 nflverse play-by-play. Load it with `ep_load(season, type="weekly")`; it returns about 159 columns with `*_exp` names. Expect some drift since training ended in 2020, so recalibrate.
- **xTD:** stickier year to year than raw TDs (r 0.60 vs 0.52) and at least as predictive of next season's TDs. Big TD overperformers regress hard: of flex players with 10+ TDs, only 8.5% gained the next year, and the average drop was about 5.5 TDs.
- **Season-to-season stability (published):** WR target share r ≈ 0.73 (0.55 in another study); TE target share ≈ 0.70; route share ≈ 0.58; targets per route run (TPRR) ≈ 0.65.
- **Within-season stability:** no published figures exist for when in-season metrics settle. Estimate it in-house (split-half reliability), then shrink each metric toward its preseason prior: weight = n/(n+k), with k fitted per metric.
- **Data gap:** free in-season data has snap counts but not routes or coverage type. nflverse participation data (routes, man/zone) arrives only after the season ends.

### 4. Matchups
- **Defense vs. position is a weak signal.** Fantasy points allowed repeat only modestly year to year (QB r ≈ 0.26–0.27, TE ≈ 0.16, WR near zero). Week-to-week defensive signals are about 0.05–0.10.
- **Method:** opponent-adjust (relative to what each offense normally scores), shrink heavily toward a prior, and cap the adjustment.
- **Vegas implied team totals** should be the main environment signal. A 2026 Princeton thesis (single, unreviewed) found Vegas features were 40–52% of model feature importance.
- **CB shadow matchups:** PFF found shadowed WRs averaged only +0.24 points vs. expectation across 35 corners. Shadows are rare and rarely cover 100% of routes.
- **Man/zone splits:** I found no evidence they are predictive.

### 5. Injuries (what can actually be estimated)
**Supportable:**
- **P(play):** Questionable players play about 71% of the time and Doubtful players about 6–7% (Footballguys, 2017–2025). By final practice status: Full 86%, Limited 71%, Did Not Participate 29%. Some teams overuse the Questionable tag.
- **Hamstring:** 38.4% of NFL hamstring injuries are recurrences and 11.9% recur in the same season. Risk peaks when a player returns within 2 weeks.
- **ACL:** only 19% of players regained pre-injury fantasy output; WRs averaged about −16% fantasy points per game in the year after.
- **Concussion:** no significant performance drop for RBs/WRs who returned.

**Mixed evidence:** performance while playing through a Questionable tag ranges from −18% to no change, depending on the study. Expert projections already account for injuries, so don't discount twice.

**Not supportable:** predicting a specific player's chance of getting hurt, "injury-prone" scores, or exact return dates from vague reports.

**Data gaps:** nflverse injury data was reported broken for 2025 (needs checking). Day-by-day practice progression isn't in nflverse, so start archiving Sleeper's player injury and practice fields daily.

### 6. Trades and waivers
- **Replacement level:** use the best actual free agent at each position in the imported league. That is better than formula baselines.
- **Trade value:** gain in my optimal lineup over the rest of the season, week by week, including byes and with playoff weeks weighted (from `playoff_week_start`). Then run a season simulation for playoff and title odds before and after. Show fairness (market value, e.g., FantasyCalc for dynasty) separately from "helps you."
- **FAAB:** Sleeper's processed transactions keep `waiver_bid` even on failed claims, so the league's runner-up bids can be seen (needs checking against real data). Bid recommendation = predicted runner-up bid + ε, capped by what the player is worth to the user.
- **Sleeper trending adds** mainly predict how much competition there will be for a player, not his value.

### 7. Validation and explanations
- **Start a weekly snapshot archive of every input now** (GitHub Actions cron writing JSON). Free historical projection archives are scarce, and every week not saved is lost backtest data.
- **Backtesting:** walk forward week by week, using only data available as of each Sunday morning. Score on a player pool of top-N projected plus top-N actual, as FantasyPros does.
- **Metrics:** mean absolute error (MAE) by position, Spearman rank correlation, interval coverage and pinball loss, and Brier score / log loss for P(play) and win probability.
- **Explanations:** start with deterministic factor breakdowns. If an LLM is added later, it only narrates a JSON of computed numbers, and its output is checked against those numbers.

### Net assessment
The methodology itself is sound and appropriately conservative. Its core arithmetic holds up: the win-probability numbers reproduce exactly, the Monte Carlo SE is correct, and so is the implied-total formula. Three conclusions held up under independent checks on 2026 data: the Questionable base rate is roughly 68-70%, Doubtful players almost never play, and CV depends on the projected mean. The data-availability claims are mostly right but several are stale. nflverse injuries are working again in 2026 through a new NFL-API pipeline, though the schema lost date_modified and the feed updates once a day. 2025 participation (man/zone) data is already public. The ecr_type codes are wrong ('wsf'/'dsf', not 'sf'). FantasyPros' weekly ECR already includes r2p_pts. The ffopportunity in-season cadence is now confirmed (2026 weeks 1-5 present).

Important limitation of this check: the shared web-search budget was exhausted on my first query. I therefore verified against primary-source GitHub repos and downloaded nflverse data, plus my own computations, and I could not check any of the blog or medical effect sizes. These are FFA R², the Princeton thesis numbers, PFF shadow +0.24, the 4for4 defense correlations, xTD r values, and the hamstring and ACL rates. Treat all of them as directional, not exact. The hamstring 38.4% figure in particular conflicts with the commonly cited 16.5% NFL figure and should be traced to its source.

The plan should handle three points with caution. First, FAAB losing-bid observability (waiver_bid on failed Sleeper claims) has only test-fixture evidence and needs a live-league check before the FAAB model depends on it. Second, the P(play) model must combine designation with practice status rather than stack separate base rates, and it needs a game-day freshness layer beyond nflverse. Third, the CC-BY-SA share-alike terms on ffopportunity and FTN data apply to any redistributed archive or cache.

- [medium LB] **Consensus aggregation**: Blended (averaged) projections beat every single source; simple mean was about as good as an accuracy-weighted mean and slightly better than a robust (outlier-downweighted) mean. — Fantasy Football Analytics 2016 update: average, weighted and robust averages each beat CBS, NFL.com, ESPN etc. in every year studied. A weighted average only helps if some analysts are consistently better. These are blog analyses on small samples, not peer-reviewed. Implication: equal-weight mean of independent sources is the default; trimmed mean only when there are 5 or more sources. (https://r-bloggers.com/?p=117620)
- [high LB] **Consensus aggregation**: Forecast-combination theory supports equal weights: weights estimated from finite samples usually lose to the simple average when true optimal weights are near-equal. — Smith & Wallis (2009), Oxford Bulletin of Economics and Statistics, 'A Simple Explanation of the Forecast Combination Puzzle'. Practical rule: learn per-source accuracy weights only after a full season or more of your own archived data, and shrink them toward equal (e.g., w = (1-λ)/K + λ·w_inverse_MSE with λ ≤ 0.3). Also ignore error covariances when estimating weights. Down-weight sources that are near-duplicates of each other (shared data vendors), because correlated sources make the consensus look more certain than it is. (https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:obuest:v:71:y:2009:i:3:p:331-355)
- [medium LB] **Consensus aggregation**: Projections are roughly twice as accurate as rankings (R² ≈ 0.53 vs 0.27 in an FFA comparison), so point projections should be the primary consensus object and ranks a secondary signal. — Ranks carry no magnitude, so a gap of 1 rank can mean 0.1 or 4 points. Convert ranks to points with a position-week curve: the k-th ranked player gets the k-th highest aggregated projection at that position that week. Without same-week projections, fall back on a historical isotonic fit of actual points vs ECR rank by position. Use ECR sd/best/worst as a disagreement/uncertainty feature. (https://www.r-bloggers.com/2016/04/which-are-more-accurate-fantasy-football-rankings-or-projections/)
- [high LB] **Consensus aggregation (inputs)**: FantasyPros ECR is computed from 'Rank Points', not average rank. - [medium LB] **Projection sources / betting markets**: A 2026 Princeton senior thesis, trained on 32,900 player-weeks (2022-2025), beat ESPN's weekly projections by a small margin (MAE 2.909 vs 3.074). Vegas-derived features were 40-52% of feature gain, and much of the edge came from implicitly modeling whether a player plays at all. — Single unreviewed thesis: per-position XGBoost (RB/WR/TE), quantile regression for 80% intervals, and beat ESPN in all 18 weeks of 2025 when ensembled. Three implications: (a) Vegas lines/props are the most valuable non-expert input; (b) a P(play) component is a big lever; (c) realistic gains over a good baseline are about 5% MAE, not dramatic. MAE values depend heavily on the player pool (including low-volume players shrinks MAE). (https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/d6981488-f30c-47ce-8f06-b6c3e98f2578)
- [medium] **Projection sources / betting markets**: Player-prop lines sit systematically below fantasy-site projections because props price in injury and benching risk and are closer to medians than means. No rigorous public head-to-head of props vs expert projections was found. — CBS (Heath Cummings) notes props are 'universally lower'. Yardage distributions are right-skewed, so mean > median. To use props as a consensus source: de-vig the over/under prices, treat the line as a median conditional on playing, then convert to a mean using a position-specific skew factor fitted from history. Vendor claims (4for4, FantasyLabs) about which is more accurate are self-interested. (https://www.cbssports.com/fantasy/football/news/fantasy-football-expert-vs-nfl-prop-odds-eight-rbs-projected-to-smash-overunders-in-betting-market)
- [medium LB] **Projection distributions**: Weekly coefficient of variation (CV) is lowest for QBs and rises RB < WR < TE. One source gives RB 0.5-0.6, WR 0.6-0.7, TE 0.7-0.8; another gives QB 41%, RB 63%, WR 61%, TE 70% at a 10-point average. — CV rises as the mean falls, so model σ as a function of (position, projected mean), e.g. σ = a_pos + b_pos·μ, fitted on projection residuals rather than each player's own weekly SD. A player's own SD comes from too few games, is contaminated by role changes, and is TD-driven. Weekly scoring is right-skewed with a point mass near zero, so the normal-distribution rule of thumb (floor = μ − SD) is wrong in the tails. Use a mixture: P(inactive)·0 + P(active)·(empirical or quantile-regression residual distribution), plus an early-exit component. (https://playerprofiler.com/?p=74233 ; https://www.4for4.com/2016/preseason/leveraging-weekly-player-consistency-daily-fantasy-football)
- [medium] **Projection distributions / correlation**: Reported weekly correlations: QB-WR1 about 0.46 (4for4) to 0.55 (FantasyLabs, since 2014). QB+WR2 and QB+TE1 are somewhat lower, and QB vs the opposing DST is the strongest negative pair. — These come from DFS-industry studies (2018-2022) with different windows, so treat them as priors and re-estimate from nflverse weekly data. Implementation: a game-level factor model (player = μ + σ·(√ρ·Z_team_game + √(1-ρ)·ε)) or a Gaussian copula over empirical marginals. For head-to-head (H2H) fantasy matchups, correlations matter mostly when your starters share a team with the opponent's starters (hedging) or with each other (stacking). (https://www.4for4.com/2021/preseason/using-4for4-nfl-dfs-player-correlation-tool ; https://fantasylabs.com/?p=3966)
- [high LB] **Start/sit: win probability vs expected points**: In a typical PPR head-to-head matchup, choosing a high-variance over a low-variance player with equal means changes weekly win probability by only about +0.2 to +0.7 percentage points. Giving up even 0.5 projected points for variance pays only when you are a 20+ point underdog. — Own calculation (normal approximation): 9 starters per side, each SD 7.5, gives a team-score difference with SD ≈ 31.8 points. Swapping an SD-5 player for an SD-9 player: underdog by 10 goes 37.48% → 37.81%; by 30 goes 16.91% → 17.58%. With a −1.0 point mean sacrifice it is negative unless the deficit is about 30 or more. Theory (underdogs should seek variance) is in arXiv:1111.0693, but no fantasy-specific empirical study of the edge was found. Conclusion: default to max expected points with P(play) built in; use win probability as a tiebreaker and display metric. The user's vision overstates how much this lever matters. (training knowledge (own calculation) ; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1111.0693)
- [high LB] **Start/sit: Monte Carlo design**: A 10,000-iteration Monte Carlo has about 0.5 percentage point standard error on a 50% probability, the same size as the lineup edges being compared. — SE = sqrt(p(1-p)/n): 1k → 1.6pp, 10k → 0.5pp, 100k → 0.16pp. Comparing candidate lineups with independent simulations will flip recommendations on noise. Use common random numbers (the same draws for every candidate lineup) or an analytic normal/skew-normal approximation, and run it in a Web Worker. Lineup enumeration is small (flex/superflex combinations), so brute force is fine. (training knowledge (own calculation))
- [high] **Lineup optimization (academic precedent)**: Peer-reviewed or archival precedents exist. Hunter, Vielma & Zaman (arXiv:1604.01455) use integer programming that maximizes expected score subject to variance and correlation constraints, with a closed-form Gaussian win-probability approximation (DFS). Becker & Sun (JQAS 2016, 12(1):17-30) use a robust mixed integer program for season-long draft, weekly lineup and free-agent decisions. — Becker & Sun trained on 2004-2006 and simulated 2007-2008, reporting an edge over conventional strategy. Hunter et al. target top-heavy DFS payouts, which differ from H2H. Both are useful design references; neither quantifies the H2H win-probability edge over max expected points. Trade value should be the change in the team's optimal-lineup points over the rest of the season, not the sum of player values. — ROS value per week w = Σ over optimal-lineup slots of P(active)·E[pts], computed with and without the trade, accounting for byes. Weight playoff weeks (from Sleeper settings.playoff_week_start, typically 15-17; field name from training knowledge) by P(make playoffs). Then Monte Carlo the remaining schedule (Sleeper matchups give opponents) for playoff and title odds before and after. Report two numbers: 'helps you' (Δ title odds or Δ lineup points) and 'fairness' (Δ value over replacement for both sides, or market values). For dynasty, blend in FantasyCalc values (an API such as api.fantasycalc.com/values/current?isDynasty=true&numQbs=1&numTeams=12&ppr=1 is from training knowledge and needs verification). KeepTradeCut has no official API. (training knowledge ; https://ideas.repec.Org/a/bpj/jqsprt/v12y2016i1p17-30n1.html)
- [medium LB] **Waivers/FAAB**: Sleeper's league transactions endpoint (/v1/league/<league_id>/transactions/<round>) returns processed waivers. Third-party projects report that failed claims still carry settings.waiver_bid, so losing bids are observable, while pending claims are invisible until processed. — This lets the app see each league's actual bidding market: for each contested player, the winning and runner-up bids. Fit a simple model, e.g. log(bid share of budget) ~ player value rank + week + bidders' remaining budgets. Recommend bid = min(my value-based max, predicted 2nd-highest bid + ε). Sleeper deducts the winner's own bid (first-price), per its FAAB help docs (training knowledge). Verify field behavior against a live league; small leagues give sparse data early, so fall back to cross-league priors. (https://docs.sleeper.com/ ; https://github.com/keenengvang/msgffl/pull/22 ; https://github.com/gtonic/nfl_mcp/pull/199 ; https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/1876040-how-does-faab-bidding-work)
- [medium] **Waivers/FAAB: crowd signal**: Sleeper's trending endpoint (/v1/players/nfl/trending/add?lookback_hours=24&limit=25; drop is also available) is a popularity and news signal. No study was found showing it predicts player value beyond projections; it is better used to predict demand (how contested and expensive a pickup will be). — Waiver score = Δ ROS optimal-lineup value for this specific roster (league-aware replacement) + opportunity shock (starter injury via depth chart → backup's expected share) + shrunk usage trend; trending adds feed the bid estimate. RotoWire's FAAB efficiency metric (winning vs runner-up bid) is a useful evaluation metric; bid sizing is league-specific. (https://docs.sleeper.com/ ; https://www.rotowire.com/baseball/article/collette-calls-can-we-be-more-efficient-at-faab-72125 ; https://fantasyindex.com/2024/04/10/analysis/the-art-of-faab)
- [high LB] **Validation**: FantasyPros' accuracy methodology is a good template: snapshot rankings at Thursday kickoff and at 1pm ET Sunday, lock Thursday players, use Half-PPR, and score a pool of ECR top-N plus actual top-N per position so breakouts and busts are both scored. — Backtest design: walk-forward by week on 2021-2025 using only as-of-Sunday-morning information (final injury designations, not actual inactives; nflverse stat corrections applied only to targets). Metrics: MAE/RMSE by position on that pool, within-position Spearman correlation, 80% interval coverage plus pinball loss (CRPS) for distributions, Brier score/log loss with reliability bins for P(play) and win probability, and start/sit decision accuracy on contested pairs versus ECR and Sleeper projections as baselines. Pre-register success thresholds before looking at results. (https://fantasypros.com/about/faq/football-inseason-accuracy-methodology)
- [medium LB] **Validation: historical data gap**: Free archives of historical weekly projections from multiple sources are scarce, so a rigorous consensus backtest needs the app to start archiving now. The undocumented Sleeper projections endpoint may serve past weeks (unverified). — Set up a GitHub Actions cron that snapshots every input (projections, ECR, Sleeper injury/practice fields, trending, lines) to versioned JSON at fixed times (e.g., Thu, Sat, Sun 11am ET) and commits it. These archives become both the backtest set and the public accuracy dashboard. The ffanalytics R package (avg_type average/robust/weighted, add_uncertainty) is a reference implementation of multi-source aggregation. (training knowledge ; https://github.com/FantasyFootballAnalytics/ffanalytics)
- [high] **Explanation layer**: Every recommendation should come with a deterministic factor breakdown (consensus base, scoring rescore, P(play), usage trend, matchup/Vegas adjustment, variance) that sums to the final number. An LLM, if used, should only narrate that JSON. — Grounding pattern: pass the computed factors and their sources as structured input; the prompt forbids introducing facts or numbers not in the payload; post-check that every number in the output appears in the payload; fall back to template text if the check fails. Show confidence and disagreement (ECR sd, source spread) prominently. On a static GitHub Pages app, LLM calls need a user-supplied key or a serverless proxy, which is out of scope here. (training knowledge)

## RECOMMENDATIONS
- [MVP] Build the consensus engine as an equal-weight mean of stat-level projections rescored to each league's Sleeper scoring_settings, with ECR (and its sd) as a secondary rank signal converted to points via the same-week position curve. Compute bonus/threshold scoring as expectations over a distribution. — Evidence (FFA blends; Smith & Wallis) says simple averages are hard to beat. Projections are much more accurate than ranks. Exact league rescoring is a concrete edge over generic rankings.
- [MVP] Implement a P(play) model (designation + final practice status + position + team effect) and represent every weekly projection as a mixture: P(inactive)·0 + P(active)·distribution. — Base rates are well established (Q≈71%, D≈6-7%, FP 86/LP 71/DNP 29%). The Princeton thesis shows availability modeling drives much of the accuracy edge. This is the defensible version of 'calculated injury risk'.
- [MVP] Start/sit optimizer maximizes expected points (with P(play) mixture), places late-kickoff and Questionable players in FLEX for swap optionality, and displays an analytic win probability for the matchup. — Own calculation shows variance preference is worth <1pp per decision; max expected points captures nearly all the value, and the displayed win probability is still useful UX.
- [MVP] Compute replacement level from each league's actual free agents, and score trades and waivers by the change in the user's optimal lineup over the rest of the season (byes included, playoff weeks weighted). — League import makes this possible and it is more accurate than formula baselines. It separates 'helps you' from fairness.
- [MVP] Immediately start a GitHub Actions cron that snapshots all inputs (projections, ECR, Sleeper players/injury/practice fields, trending, lines) and all app recommendations to versioned JSON, then scores them after games. — No free historical multi-source projection archive exists. Every unarchived week is lost backtest and calibration data, and the archive powers a trust-building accuracy dashboard.
- [MVP] Use Vegas implied team totals/spreads as the primary matchup and game-environment signal, plus a small, opponent-adjusted, empirically shrunk and capped defense-vs-position adjustment. Never stack both on top of consensus projections that already include them. — Points allowed by position is weak year to year (QB r≈0.26, WR ~0); Vegas features dominate model importance; double-counting is a real risk.
- [MVP] Deterministic factor-breakdown explanations (template text) for every recommendation. — Transparency and trust without an LLM backend on static hosting; prevents invented analysis.
- [v1] Add a usage and regression module: ffopportunity xFP/xTD (recalibrated), shrunk snap/target/air-yards shares with per-metric k from in-house split-half reliability, and buy-low/sell-high flags when (actual − expected) is large but usage is stable. — xTD is stickier than TDs and TD overperformers regress hard; usage shares are the stickiest stats.
- [v1] Win-probability lineup optimization with a team-game factor correlation model and common random numbers, plus season Monte Carlo for playoff and title odds (before vs after a trade). — Adds correct tie-breaking, hedging and stacking logic and trade 'helps you' metrics. Must avoid Monte Carlo noise (10k sims ≈ 0.5pp SE).
- [v1] FAAB bid recommender trained on the league's own processed waivers (winning and losing waiver_bid values), with trending adds as a demand feature and cross-league priors early in the season. — League-specific bidding markets differ greatly; losing bids appear to be observable via Sleeper transactions.
- [v1] Publish an in-season accuracy dashboard: MAE by position vs Sleeper/ECR baselines, Brier score for P(play) and win probability, interval coverage. — Holds the 'most efficient analyst' claim to measurable standards and catches model drift.
- [v2] Floor/ceiling via a position-specific, heteroscedastic quantile model (σ as a function of projected mean) trained on the app's own archived projection-vs-actual residuals. — The normal ±SD rule is wrong given skew and zero mass; player-specific SD is too noisy.
- [v2] Allow accuracy-weighted consensus only after a full season or more of archived data, with weights shrunk heavily toward equal and duplicate-vendor sources down-weighted. — Forecast combination puzzle: estimated weights rarely beat equal weights on small samples.
- [v2] Injury-history priors: same-season hamstring recurrence (higher risk within 2 weeks of return), ACL year-one efficiency discount, empirical games-missed distributions by injury type, all shown with wide intervals. — Supported by NFL-specific studies but small effects and noisy reporting; useful context, not a driver.
- [v2] Optional LLM narration that receives only the computed factor JSON and is post-checked for numeric fidelity. — Better UX without hallucinated analysis; requires a key/proxy strategy for static hosting.
- [v2] Dynasty mode: blend FantasyCalc market values (verify API) with ROS lineup value; show fairness and contender/rebuilder fit separately. — Market values capture long-term value that ROS projections ignore.
- [later] WR/CB shadow and man/zone adjustments only as an opt-in, capped (a few percent), clearly labeled experimental feature, and only with paid alignment data and a backtest showing lift. — Evidence shows near-zero average effect (PFF +0.24 xFP), rare true shadows, no evidence that man/zone splits predict, and free coverage data only after the season.
- [avoid] Do not ship player-specific 'injury risk %' or 'injury-prone' scores, or matchup ranks from raw unadjusted points allowed. — Not supportable with public data; would give false precision and erode trust.
- [avoid] Do not use an LLM to generate analysis, rankings or picks. — Ungrounded analysis contradicts the 'calculated assessment' value proposition and cannot be validated.

## RISKS
- [high] Licensing/ToS of consensus inputs (FantasyPros ECR, site projections, KTC, odds) may block redistributing 'expert consensus from all locations' in a public app. — mitigation: Coordinate with the data-sources research; prefer licensed/API sources and open data (nflverse, Sleeper); build the methodology so it degrades gracefully with fewer sources; never scrape against ToS.
- [high] False precision: showing win probabilities, injury risks or CB matchup effects with more certainty than the evidence supports. — mitigation: Show intervals and disagreement, cap weak adjustments, label experimental features, and publish calibration (Brier, coverage) from the accuracy archive.
- [high] No historical projection archive, so consensus quality and adjustments cannot be validated or calibrated. — mitigation: Start the GitHub Actions snapshot archive now (Week 7 2026); backtest P(play)/usage/defense models on nflverse 2017-2025 in the meantime; check Sleeper historical projections availability.
- [medium] Double-counting: applying injury, matchup or Vegas adjustments on top of expert projections that already include them. — mitigation: Each adjustment must be estimated as residual lift over consensus in backtests; only apply where (actual − consensus) shows a significant signal.
- [medium] Free in-season data gaps: nflverse injuries reportedly broken for 2025; routes and man/zone coverage available only after the season; ffopportunity in-season update cadence unknown. — mitigation: Verify each feed at build time; archive Sleeper player injury/practice fields daily; use snap share and target/air-yards shares from play-by-play in-season; keep route-based metrics optional (paid).
- [medium] ffopportunity models trained on 2006-2020 may be miscalibrated for current NFL scoring environments. — mitigation: Check season-level expected vs actual totals; apply a linear recalibration per stat; or retrain a simpler xFP in a scheduled Python job.
- [medium] Monte Carlo noise flipping lineup recommendations (10k sims ≈ 0.5pp SE, the same size as true edges). — mitigation: Use common random numbers or analytic approximations; only override a max-expected-points lineup when the win-probability gain clearly exceeds simulation error.
- [medium] Overfitting per-source weights, per-player variance, or matchup multipliers on small samples. — mitigation: Equal weights by default; empirical-Bayes shrinkage with k estimated from split-half reliability; walk-forward validation only.
- [low] Static hosting (GitHub Pages) cannot run heavy server-side jobs on demand. — mitigation: Precompute league-agnostic artifacts (consensus stat lines, P(play), defense ratings, xFP) in scheduled GitHub Actions as JSON; run league-specific rescoring, optimization and simulations client-side in Web Workers.
- [medium] User expectations (CB/scheme matchup engine, injury prediction) exceed what the evidence supports. — mitigation: Set expectations in the plan: frame features as 'availability probability' and 'game environment'; show the evidence strength for each factor in the UI.
